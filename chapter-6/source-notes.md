# Source basis and study guide

This 150-item bank uses the supplied Chapter 6 pasted text, the supplied 18-page HCA200 Day 6 slideshow, and the newly supplied HCA200 Day 6 Chapter 6 Quiz Source Brief. The brief is a synthesis of lecture notes, not an independently verified transcript; instructor examples are treated as examples rather than universal rules. The original linked chat returned only five recent study-song exchanges and no older cursor. All original lecture transcripts could not be recovered directly. The new brief substantially improves instructor coverage but does not remove that retrieval limit.

The seven-step cycle diagram was extracted from the original slideshow and visually inspected. Its sequence is: choosing and allocating time to reflect; choosing a challenging or interesting event; describing and exploring the chosen event; exploring all reactions of self and Person; establishing relevant goals for similar future events or interactions; developing strategies and/or future action plans; acting to fulfil the plans, reflecting upon and changing healthcare as necessary. Quiz labels are clear paraphrases of this verified sequence. The arrows return to the beginning.

Johns’s seven-part examination was checked against the supplied chapter: description and contributing factors; possible causes of reactions and interaction purpose; significant background/environment/individual information; aims and reasons for actions; consequences including each person’s feelings; alternatives, why not chosen and possible consequences; resultant learning and future reactions. It is distinct from the cycle despite also having seven elements.

The slide’s dictionary-style suppression wording is imprecise for a defence mechanism. Questions instead use the chapter’s temporary, partly conscious postponement and return, reinforced by the new instructor brief. Reflective and reflexive practice are complementary: the bank includes retrospective learning as well as examination of the worker’s assumptions, position, and influence, including during interaction. Altruism, sublimation, suppression, anticipation, and humour receive substantially more emphasis than other mechanisms. Other mechanisms remain for contrast because the user requested them, although the brief says their diagnostic categories were not instructor priorities. Chapter category labels are not used to diagnose individuals. The chapter’s grouping of isolation of affect with intellectualisation is treated as its terminology.

The brief supplies additional applications: caregiver gender preference; shower, wound-care and dietary refusal; disability versus incapacity; personal needs and home-care boundaries; tone and de-escalation; keys/phone and mentorship as safety examples; objective advocacy; care-plan use; confidentiality and unauthorized recording; treatment preferences and current authorized orders; expected/unexpected death; and professional accountability. These are tested with scope, supervision and workplace-procedure limits. Possible complications are not made inevitable. A reported preference is not treated as an independently changed order. No universal autopsy rule or immunity from liability is asserted. This bank does not independently establish jurisdiction-specific clinical or legal requirements.

The instructor’s tentative estimate of about 20 questions in 25 minutes is not treated as a confirmed exam format. This is a much larger, more demanding study bank, not a predicted official exam.

## How to practise

All 150 questions now use answer choices: 112 multiple-choice and 38 true/false items. Questions 135–150 are integrated multiple-choice scenarios converted from the earlier written exercises. The first 134 questions retain their original content. Each correct answer earns one point; maximum 150. No score is a validated course pass threshold.

The Study Arcade includes topic rounds, mixed rounds of up to 20 questions, explanations, saved progress and a retry queue. There are no write-in fields. Questions and answer choices are shuffled for practice. Progress is saved in this browser; it does not sync between devices. Use the separate question bank and answer key for offline study. Answer data is included for teaching feedback, so this is not a secure examination.

## Quality review

Items test content rather than source locations. Correct answers were checked against the recovered materials. Scenarios distinguish observation from inference, explicit facts from hypotheses, and professional action from unsupported authority. Distractors were reviewed for competing best answers and wording was checked for grammar, coherent actors, and unnecessary absolutes. This is a generated practice resource, not an official examination.
