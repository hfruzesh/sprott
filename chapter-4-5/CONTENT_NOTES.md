# Content review notes

## Coverage

- Chapter 4: ethics vs. morals; beliefs and values; employer codes; autonomy, justice, beneficence and nonmaleficence; professional boundaries; client-centred ethical decision-making; culture and personal bias; scenarios involving refusal, family requests, discretion, alcohol, and professional relationships.
- Chapter 5: moral and legal rights; Charter and provincial/territorial human-rights protections; dignity and DIPPS; privacy and electronic privacy; informed consent and withdrawal; substitute decision makers, advance directives and estate roles; criminal/civil law; torts and negligence; scope; human rights, OH&S, WHMIS, employment standards, labour relations and workers' compensation.

## Content decisions

- Questions assess knowledge and application, never ask learners to identify a chapter, page, slide, or source organization.
- The four ethics principles are kept distinct: autonomy (choice), justice (fairness), beneficence (doing/promoting good), and nonmaleficence (doing no harm).
- The Chapter 5 distinction between assault (threat/attempt that causes fear of bodily harm) and battery (actual touching without consent) is preserved.
- Advance directives and living wills are distinguished using the supplied textbook text; the materials say a living will has no legal status in Canada.
- Worker refusal rights are stated with the textbook's qualification that provincial or territorial exceptions may apply when a health-care worker's refusal would endanger a client.
- No new jurisdiction-specific laws or unsupported legal exceptions have been added.
