## Accuracy choices

- Chapter 2 coverage is deliberately weighted toward Therese’s quiz-review topics, while retaining chapter-level breadth across system structure, history, funding, Indigenous health, service delivery, prevention and current challenges.
- Chapter 3 preserves the course distinction between community-based and site-based services. A residence is not automatically classified as a site-based health-care service.
- Assisted living is not described as providing 24-hour professional nursing. Long-term care is described as providing 24-hour nursing.
- Palliative care is distinguished from end-of-life care: Chapter 3 defines palliative care by its quality-of-life and symptom-management goal for incurable illness, while end-of-life care is described for people who have stopped treatment to cure or control disease.
- Chapter 3 identifies hospice as palliative care for a client with a terminal diagnosis who is no longer seeking life-prolonging care; it may be provided at home, in a live-in setting or as inpatient care.
- Retirement homes are one type of assisted-living facility, so questions avoid treating these labels as mutually exclusive. Group homes and retirement homes may be live-in facilities and also be described as community-based services; prompts specify whether they test the residence or service model.
- Staffing counts, eligibility rules, legal requirements and universal policies were not added as facts.
- No textbook pages or long passages are reproduced. Question wording and explanations are original.
