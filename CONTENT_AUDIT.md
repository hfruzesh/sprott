# Content coverage and audit notes

## Scope

The source is the user's 47-slide PowerPoint. The title slide (1) is identified in the app and is not tested. All instructional/resource slides 2–47 have at least one cited question. The bank contains 155 questions and the same number of flashcards across nine navigation topics.

| Slides | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| 2–6 | Section map, program purpose and outcomes, Registry |
| 7–9 | Infection prevention, hand hygiene, PPE |
| 10–20 | Why terminology matters; prefixes; roots; suffixes; directions; abbreviations |
| 21–24 | Skeletal, muscular, integumentary, sensory, cardiovascular, lymphatic, respiratory, gastrointestinal, endocrine, nervous, urinary and reproductive systems; deck-reported fun facts |
| 25–29 | Body mechanics, posture, lifting, turning, movement, prevention and cited resources |
| 30–35 | Communication process, barriers, listening modes, nonverbal and assertive/verbal communication |
| 36–39 | Critical-thinking definition, prompts, decision process, reflection, learning from colleagues, diagram concepts |
| 40–41 | ProQuest and SPECO |
| 42–47 | Pre-learning checklist timeline, G2 resources, HCAPED/HCAST, Book Club activity and references |

## Validation method

- Extracted slide text directly from the PPTX package and mapped every question to its numbered source slide.
- Checked terminology definitions and all listed body systems against the corresponding slide text.
- Confirmed each question has four distinct choices, one keyed answer, and three choice-specific explanations for distractors.
- Preserved deck-reported numeric claims as attributed claims rather than substituting outside figures.
- Checked JavaScript syntax and the 45-question exam selection size and without-replacement logic during local verification.

## Known source limitations kept visible

Some slide statistics carry dated citations or are presented without enough sourcing to verify them from the deck alone. The app labels those items as slide-reported claims. It does not silently update them or represent them as current clinical guidance. Slide 39 contains apparent typographic errors (“OPENESS,” “PROBEM”); the study item uses the evident intended terms “openness” and “problem solving.”
